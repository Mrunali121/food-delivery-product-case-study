# QuickBite — Reorder & Live ETA Confidence
### A Product Case Study: Problem → Research → Decision → Prioritization → Solution → Measurement

**Live Site:** https://mrunali121.github.io/food-delivery-product-case-study/ 

**Role:** Product Manager (simulated project)
**Product:** QuickBite — food delivery app, operating in Germany

## About this repository

This case study follows one product problem end-to-end, the way a PM actually
works it: starting from a business-critical metric, through research that
identifies root causes, a prioritization framework that turns findings into a
defensible decision, a shipped solution, and — critically — the measurement
that proves (or would have disproven) it worked.

## The arc, at a glance

```
PROBLEM          68% of first-time customers never reorder within 30 days;
                  "Where is my order?" is 22% of all support tickets
                  ↓
RESEARCH          15 interviews, 600 tagged tickets, 412-respondent survey,
                  90-day funnel review → two root causes found, not guessed
                  ↓
DECISION           Competitor gap analysis confirms the research findings
                  aren't speculative — two rivals already ship what's missing
                  ↓
PRIORITIZATION     6 candidate features scored with RICE, live formulas,
                  workshopped with Engineering/Design/Support
                  ↓
SOLUTION            One-Tap Reorder + Live ETA Confidence — a full PRD,
                  user stories, and wireframes for the two winning bets
                  ↓
MEASUREMENT        Before/after SQL queries + charts against the exact
                  targets the PRD committed to
```

## Prioritization at a glance

RICE scoring (Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort) ranked all 6 candidates the
team considered — not just the two that shipped:

| Rank | Feature | RICE Score | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Live ETA Confidence | 28.8 | ✅ Q2 — Built |
| 2 | One-Tap Reorder | 25.5 | ✅ Q2 — Built |
| 3 | Checkout Simplification | 5.6 | Deferred to Q3 |
| 4 | Loyalty & Rewards Program | 3.0 | Deferred to Q4 |
| 5 | In-App Courier Chat | 0.4 | Not prioritized |
| 6 | Group Ordering / Bill Splitting | 0.3 | Not prioritized |

See [`feature-prioritization.xlsx`](feature-prioritization.xlsx) for the full
workbook — every score is a live formula, not a hardcoded number, so the model
is auditable and re-runnable if the underlying estimates change.

## Measured outcome

| Metric | Before | After | Target | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30-day repeat order rate | 32% | 47% | 45% | ✅ Beat target |
| Avg. absolute ETA error | 14.2 min | 4.6 min | ±5 min | ✅ Beat target |
| "Where is my order?" tickets (% of total) | 22% | 11.2% | 12% | ✅ Beat target |

Full queries in [`analytics/analysis.sql`](analytics/analysis.sql); charts

## Why this structure
It's easy to show a PRD and call it a case study. What's harder — and what
actually demonstrates product judgment — is showing the **evidence trail**
underneath a decision: why these two features and not the other four
candidates, what the team explicitly chose *not* to build this cycle, and
whether the bet actually paid off once shipped. That trail is the point of
this repository, not any single document in it.
