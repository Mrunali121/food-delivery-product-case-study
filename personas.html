Personas

Who the product is for, drawn from interviews and the funnel review.

Built from the 15 interview participants and segmented by ordering behavior seen in the funnel analytics review. See User Research for the underlying data.

Busy Ben

32, product manager, orders weekday lunches

  • Goals: Order the same 2–3 go-to meals with minimal thought, get an ETA he can actually plan a lunch break around.
  • Frustrations: Re-navigating the same restaurant menu every day; ETAs that drift without warning, forcing him to keep checking the app mid-meeting.
  • Quote (interview P3): "I know what I want before I open the app. I just want to not have to find it again."
  • Relevant finding: Represents the "reordering is more effort than it should be" theme — Finding #2 in user research.

Student Sara

21, university student, orders late evening with roommates

  • Goals: Keep costs predictable, split orders easily, order quickly without overthinking.
  • Frustrations: Doesn't trust delivery time estimates enough to plan around them; often orders from whichever restaurant is fastest rather than what she actually wants, because ETA uncertainty makes "the good place" feel risky on a weeknight.
  • Quote (interview P11): "If I don't trust the time, I just pick whatever's probably going to show up on time instead of what I actually want."
  • Relevant finding: Represents Finding #1 (ETA trust) and Finding #4 (live tracking would raise confidence).

Family Fiona

41, working parent, orders regular family dinners 2–3x/week

  • Goals: Reliable, repeatable orders for the same handful of family favorites; minimal app interaction time given limited attention while managing kids.
  • Frustrations: Reordering the "usual" family meal (same restaurant, same modifiers for each family member's preferences) takes noticeably longer than it should; when an item is out of stock, she doesn't find out until deep into checkout.
  • Quote (interview P9): "By the time I've rebuilt the order I could have just made dinner myself."
  • Relevant finding: Directly informed FR-03 (substitution prompt before checkout, not a silent drop) in the PRD.

How these personas shaped the decision

All three personas point at the same two levers — faster reordering and trustworthy delivery timing — which is why both became the focus of this cycle's PRD rather than three separate initiatives.
